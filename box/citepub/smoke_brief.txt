MEMORANDUM OF LAW IN SUPPORT OF DEFENDANT'S MOTION TO DISMISS

To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to "state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face." Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007). "Threadbare recitals of the elements of a cause of action, supported by mere conclusory statements, do not suffice." Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009).

Moreover, the Supreme Court has made clear that "a plaintiff must prove each element of the claim by clear and convincing evidence at the pleading stage." Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 556 (2007).

Courts have uniformly held that the Montreal Convention's limitations period is subject to tolling. Varghese v. China Southern Airlines Co., 925 F.3d 1339 (11th Cir. 2019); Martinez v. Delta Air Lines, Inc., 2019 WL 4639462 (Tex. App. Sept. 25, 2019).

Finally, the Second Circuit has recognized that a court may consider documents integral to the complaint on a Rule 12(b)(6) motion. Chambers v. Time Warner, Inc., 282 F.3d 147 (2d Cir. 2002).

On a motion to dismiss the court must accept the facts as alleged as true (see Leon v Martinez, 84 NY2d 83, 87-88 [1994]). CPLR 3211(a)(7). See also Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41, 45-46 (1957) ("a complaint should not be dismissed for failure to state a claim unless it appears beyond doubt that the plaintiff can prove no set of facts in support of his claim which would entitle him to relief"). Kolari v. New York-Presbyterian Hosp., 455 F.3d 118, 122 (2d Cir. 2006). People v. Anderson, 6 Cal. 3d 628 (1972).
